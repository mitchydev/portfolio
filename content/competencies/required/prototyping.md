### Competency Demonstration

To answer the design question of what the "path of least resistance" looks like for a stressed student, our prototyping phase focused entirely on efficiency. Specifically, we wanted to figure out what interaction flow would make the app the most intuitive and easy to use for someone who doesn't want to waste any extra time. To test this, we drew up digital mockups featuring various screen combinations, dropdown menus, and buttons. We then linked these designs into an interactive slideshow to act as a prototype. During our user studies, we observed how participants navigated through the slideshow to determine which layout allowed them to find or claim a room with the absolute minimum amount of friction.

These tests gave us a clear answer to our design questions: users have zero tolerance for extra steps. The slideshow studies revealed that hiding key information behind dropdown menus or complex navigation slowed students down. This confirmed our earlier findings that the most efficient design was to immediately present a direct list view upon opening the app rather than a map. By tracking how users naturally interacted with the digital mockups, we learned that prioritizing speed and minimizing taps is the only way to ensure the app feels like a seamless tool rather than an additional chore in their busy schedules.
