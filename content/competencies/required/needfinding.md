### Competency Demonstration

For our needfinding process we each tasked ourselves to conduct one interview, create one persona, and use a brainstorming method. For my approach, I first conducted a needfinding interview where I tasked a student to explain and run their process to find a study room. I observed a computer science and economics major physically outline their habitual "snake pattern" search route through the HSSC. After the observation, I interviewed them to dig into their specific frustrations with this process. I turned these insights into the "Busy and Booked CS/ECN Double Major Warrior" persona, which represents highly stressed students whose primary goal is to avoid wasting time. To generate solutions, I applied the SCAMPER brainstorming method. This technique helped me come up with ideas like using the registrar's class schedule as a baseline and eliminating unnecessary tracking locations.

This process revealed several user needs I hadn't considered beforehand. I learned that hunting for a room is a major distraction, and a failed search wastes 10 to 20 minutes right before exams. This wasted time frustrates students enough that they will often abandon the HSSC entirely and retreat to the library. I also found out that while users are perfectly willing to scan a QR code to claim a room, they actively worry about stale data caused by people forgetting to log out. What surprised me is that students don't want the gym and dining hall mappings, they just want to save time by knowing which classroom isn't booked instead of wandering aimlessly during finals and midterms week. Because students already know the predictable rush hours for food and workouts, finding an open classroom is the only true gamble.
