### How this demonstrates the competency

To figure out what students actually need, we shadowed three people on their hunt for a study room starting at Renfrow Hall, then interviewed them to compare their actions with their words. We turned these observations into three distinct personas (the Humanities Hermit, the CS/ECN Warrior, and Social Studier Sophie) and used brainstorming techniques like the "Worst Possible Idea" to identify must-have features, like real-time updates and ways to mark a room occupied without disturbing others. Crucially, we discovered that unpredictability, not just crowding, is the main pain point. A failed search wastes significant time and pushes students out of the building entirely.

User feedback directly shaped our design, proving that a simple "busy or not" status just isn't enough. Students want to know if open spots are for groups or individuals, they demand data fresher than an hour, and they prefer scanning a QR code over manual log-ins (which led us to add an automatic timeout feature to prevent stale data). The biggest surprise was realizing that "less crowded" isn't universally better. While some of us seek out totally silent rooms, others like Sophie are actually motivated by busy spaces. Because these needs vary so widely by persona, our final design has to offer nuanced space information rather than just a single room status.
